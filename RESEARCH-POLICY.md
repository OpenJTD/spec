# Shared research policy

Status: draft for joint review

Research should be independently verifiable. Each claim should identify its
evidence, inputs, reproduction procedure, and unresolved interpretation.
Keep unknown bytes and undecoded behavior explicit. A parser heuristic or
rendering fallback does not establish native format semantics.

Use public documentation, independent observation of authorized samples,
controlled experiments, and behavior observable through normal application
use. Historical implementations may corroborate an observation when their
source and use are permissible; identify that basis separately.

Do not submit proprietary source, private SDKs or internal documentation,
implementation logic reconstructed by decompilation/disassembly, or code and
materials copied without permission. Shared observations should support
independent implementation rather than require copying another engine's code.

Apache-2.0 is the default for RFCs, code, and validation material we author
jointly. External documents retain their existing terms. Record rights in
each corpus entry; neither a repository license nor DCO grants rights in
external input content or derived output.

Public corpus entries need documented redistribution rights. Private entries
need provenance and a concrete `sharing_basis` identifying why they may be
shared with the named collaborators. If a file cannot be shared, record only
the metadata and results that may be disclosed. Do not publish sensitive
content through hashes, filenames, indexes, screenshots, or expected outputs.

Publish resulting policies and research where permissible. Review private
discussion records before publication. Drafts become accepted only after an
explicit recorded joint review; implementation behavior is recorded with
its version and remains distinct from a specification claim.
